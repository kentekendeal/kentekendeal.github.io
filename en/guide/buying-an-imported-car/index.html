Buying an imported car: cheaper, but what should you check?

Many used cars in the Netherlands were first driven in Germany, Belgium or elsewhere. That can mean a good price, if you know what to look for.

How do you recognise an imported car?

The RDW data has two dates: the date of first registration (anywhere in the world) and the date of first registration in the Netherlands. If they are months or years apart, the car was first driven abroad.

KentekenDeal shows this straight away: the car gets the label Imported.

Why are imported cars often cheaper?

  • Different market: in neighbouring countries some models are cheaper, for example because there are more of them for sale.
  • Less known history: buyers pay less for a car whose past they can't check as well.
  • The same applies when you sell: what you save when buying, you often notice again when selling.

What should you check?

  1. Mileage from before the import. In the Netherlands mileage readings are registered (the NAP system), but the period abroad is harder to check. Ask for foreign service invoices or a service history and compare the readings.
  2. Damage history. Ask about previous damage and, if in doubt, have a pre-purchase inspection done. At the viewing, look extra carefully at paint differences and panel gaps.
  3. Version and options. Foreign versions can differ (other options, system language, maps). Check that everything works as you expect.
  4. Warranty and recalls. A manufacturer's warranty within the EU usually also applies in the Netherlands, but check with the dealer. Open recalls are shown in the RDW data.
  5. BPM (Dutch purchase tax). If you buy an imported car that already has a Dutch licence plate, the BPM has already been paid. If you import a used car yourself, you pay BPM when you register it in the Netherlands, and the car must first be inspected by the RDW.
Tip: always compare the asking price of an imported car with similar Dutch cars. An imported car should usually be a bit cheaper; if it costs the same, you can negotiate.

Frequently asked questions

How can I see whether a car is imported?

In the RDW data, compare the date of first registration with the date of first registration in the Netherlands. If they are far apart, the car was first driven abroad. KentekenDeal then shows the label Imported.

Do I have to pay BPM when I buy an imported car?

If the car already has a Dutch licence plate, the BPM has already been paid. If you import a used car from abroad yourself, you pay BPM on it when you register it in the Netherlands.

Is an imported car worth less?

Imported cars are often a bit cheaper to buy, and that usually also applies when you sell. The difference depends on the make, the model and how well the history can be checked.
